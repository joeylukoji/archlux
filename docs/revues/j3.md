# Review of milestone 3: performance legalization without learning

## Criterion as written

`MILESTONE-3.md` §0, three tests: every Frank-Wolfe iterate is in the polytope (drawn
from `plans_quelconques()`), the objective is non-decreasing (`plans_quelconques()`),
and `theta` and `theta + 360` give the same plan (`np.allclose(..., atol=1e-6)`).
§7: one plan, 8 orientations, a figure, and "a Rayleigh test on the gain per
orientation".

## Replayed?

Yes, on revision `040101a` and later.

- The closing tests (`test_acceptation_jalon3.py`) drew `plans_valides()` under a
  context **without minimum areas**, the case where Frank-Wolfe used to go below them
  (AUDIT.md §3 n°6, fixed by batch 1.2). They are replayed in
  `tests/proprietes/test_milestone3_as_written.py` on arbitrary plans and contexts (as
  written) and on plans with a load-bearing wall and minimum areas: 60 examples in CI,
  **2000** replayed with `ARCHLUX_GATE_EXAMPLES=2000`. Iterates are checked by the
  independent checker, not by the polytope the solver used.
- `python experiences/j3_orientation.py` (rewritten in English, 47 lines): the same
  plan at 8 azimuths, **with and without** minimum areas (12 m² per room type);
  `resultats/j3_orientation.csv` and two SVG sheets, byte-stable.

## Result

| Criterion | Arbitrary plans and contexts | Walls and minimum areas (2000) |
|---|---|---|
| every iterate valid | holds | holds |
| objective non-decreasing | holds | holds |
| `theta` and `theta + 360`: same plan | holds (one fixed two-room plan, as written) | **fails** |
| `theta` and `theta + 360`: same objective value | | holds |

**Circularity.** With a load-bearing wall at x = 1.01 and five rooms, `theta = 0` and
`theta = 360` return plans whose widths differ by 5.2 m, at the same analytic objective
(47.5176242084927 vs 47.51762420849269). The optimum is a flat face: two rooms trade
width at equal predicted daylight, and `sin(360°) = -2.4e-16` breaks the tie
differently; 1e-9° and 359.999999999° give a third plan. The plan is not a function of
the azimuth when the optimum is not unique; its value is.

**Experiment** (`resultats/j3_orientation.csv`):

| Minimum areas | Plans that move away from the classic result | Smallest room | Move vs azimuth (circular-linear fit) |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| none (as published) | 8 / 8 | **1.00 m²** (45° and 135°) | amplitude 9.0 m, peak at 57°, R² 0.45 |
| 12 m² per type | 8 / 8 | 12.03 m² | amplitude 6.7 m, peak at 351°, R² 0.54 |

Without minimum areas the published figure showed 1 m² rooms: the objective pushed a
room to the minimum width. With minimum areas, no room falls under its minimum.

**The Rayleigh test was ill-posed**: applied to the list of azimuths where the plan
moved, it tests the uniformity of 8 equally spaced angles, uniform by design (p = 1
whenever all 8 move), and `orient.circulaire.rayleigh` itself warns against a p-value
below about 50 observations. It is replaced by a circular-linear fit of the move on the
azimuth, reported without a p-value.

## Decision

- **Criteria 1 and 2 kept**, now tested as written and with walls and minimum areas.
- **Criterion 3 rewritten**: `theta` and `theta + 360` give the **same objective value**,
  and the same plan when the optimum is unique. Phase 3.1 normalizes the azimuth modulo
  360 at the input, which makes exact multiples bit-identical; it does not make a flat
  optimum unique. A tie-break rule (for instance the L1-closest point to the classic
  result among optimal plans) is a phase 6 question.
- **§7 Rayleigh test withdrawn** for this experiment; figure regenerated with minimum
  areas. Traced in [ADR 0002](../adr/0002-milestone-criteria-rewritten.md).
